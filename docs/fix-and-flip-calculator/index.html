The flip stress test

DealFit re-runs your flip with three fixed haircuts: ARV −5%, rehab +10% and one extra month of holding. The result is graded:

Margin of safetyMeaning
StrongStressed profit is at least half of your profit target (or $25,000 when no target is set)
TightStill profitable under stress, but with a thin cushion
FailsNet profit drops below $0 under stress

A flip earns DealFit's strongest verdict only when net profit is at least $50,000 or your target (whichever is higher), ROI is at least 15%, the maximum offer is positive, and the deal survives the stress test. How DealFit evaluates risk →

Questions

How does DealFit calculate fix and flip profit?

Net profit is the after-repair value minus selling costs, the purchase price, repairs, buying costs, holding costs and, when you model a loan, interest-only loan interest and points. ROI is net profit divided by the cash you put in, so it is a leveraged return when the deal is financed.

What is the maximum offer?

DealFit uses the 70% rule: 70% of the after-repair value minus the repair budget. If you turn on Self-Renovating, it uses 75% instead.

What does the flip stress test do?

It recalculates the same deal with the after-repair value down 5%, rehab up 10% and one extra month of holding. If net profit falls below zero, the margin of safety is Fails. It is Strong when the stressed profit is at least half of your profit target, or $25,000 when no target is set, and Tight in between.

Where does the repair estimate come from?

The repair estimator multiplies square footage by a light, mid or full rehab cost band for the selected market. It is a planning estimate; replace it with your contractor's bid when you have one.
